Why is my Google Fiber internet slow?

Slow Google Fiber internet has two possible sources: your home Wi-Fi, or Google Fiber's network. Here's how to tell them apart, the slowdowns typical of Google Fiber, and the fixes that work.

By Corey Haines, maker of WiFi or ISP · Updated October 6, 2026

The short answer

Compare your router with the internet. If the router is quick and the internet is slow, it's Google Fiber. Check for an outage, restart your equipment in the right order, and if it keeps happening, log it and contact Google Fiber with the evidence.

Rebranded from Google Fiber to GFiber (support moved from support.google.com/fiber to gfiber.com/support). No data caps, overage charges or throttling per gFiber FAQ. On March 11, 2026, GFiber and Stonepeak's Astound Broadband announced they will combine; Stonepeak will be majority owner with Alphabet a significant minority shareholder, expected close Q4 2026.

First, rule out your Wi-Fi

A slow Google Fiber connection and a slow Wi-Fi network feel identical. Time a round trip to your router and one to the internet at the same moment. If your router answers quickly and the internet doesn't, the slowdown is on Google Fiber's side. If even your router is slow, it's your Wi-Fi or router, and Google Fiber can't fix it. Here's how to check on a Mac.

What usually slows down Google Fiber

Google Fiber delivers internet over fiber (FTTH). The usual causes:

  • Wi-Fi: gFiber says it is unlikely you will hit full gigabit speeds over Wi-Fi; wired Ethernet is fastest.
  • Router placement and obstacles (concrete, metal, brick; to a lesser extent drywall, wood, thick glass) and interference from microwaves and baby monitors.
  • Older devices and browsers max out at lower speeds; multi-gig needs Wi-Fi 6E/7 capable devices.
  • VPNs: speed is capped by how fast the VPN server can process data.
  • Concurrent streaming (e.g. 4K) using much of the bandwidth.
  • Peak hours (7-11 PM) congestion on external websites and servers outside gFiber's control.
  • Poorly placed or too many mesh extenders.

Upload versus download

Symmetrical: 'your max download and upload speeds will match your service.' Plans: Core 1 Gig, Home 3 Gig (up to 3,000 Mbps both ways), Edge 8 Gig. When an upload fills that smaller pipe, everything else lags, which is called bufferbloat. Test for it.

Check for a Google Fiber outage

Google Fiber outage page

Public tool: enter your street address to see network status, no sign-in needed. Outages also appear in the Network section of the gFiber App and as a red banner in the online account portal. Text START to 434237 for SMS outage updates.

Restart your Google Fiber equipment

  1. Unplug the power cord.
  2. Wait at least 10 seconds.
  3. Plug the power cord back in.
  4. Wait up to 5 minutes for the device to turn back on (Wi-Fi 6E/Wi-Fi 7 router light goes solid green; Wi-Fi 6 router solid blue).
  5. Alternative: tap Restart router in the gFiber App and confirm.

Your Google Fiber equipment

gFiber-supplied router plus a Fiber Jack (ONT, model GFLT100). Current routers: Wi-Fi 7 Router (GRBE331C, included with Home 3 Gig and Edge 8 Gig), Wi-Fi 6E Router (GR6EXX0C, included with Core 1 Gig), Wi-Fi 6 Router (GRAX210T), Multi-Gig Router (GFRG300). Mesh extenders: Wi-Fi 7 (GEBE320C), Wi-Fi 6E (GE6E220C). Older installs may have Nest Wifi Pro, Google Wifi or the legacy Network Box (no longer supported).

Admin page: 192.168.1.1 (gFiber: the router's LAN IP is 'typically 192.168.1.1'). gFiber directs settings changes to the gFiber App or the online account portal rather than a local web page.

App: gFiber App (Google Home app also supported for network monitoring)

Bridge mode: Yes. In the gFiber App: Network > Advanced network settings > Edit router settings > toggle Bridge mode on, with your router's WAN port connected to the gFiber router's 10 Gbps LAN port. Intended for the Multi-Gig Router (GFRG300) when there is no separate wall-mounted Fiber Jack, or custom setups; it turns off the gFiber router's Wi-Fi, routing and mesh extenders. Otherwise plug your own router straight into the Fiber Jack.

Your own equipment: Yes. Connect a third-party router to the Fiber Jack's Ethernet port. gFiber recommends a 1 Gbps WAN port and Wi-Fi 6 for 1 Gig, a 10 Gbps WAN port with Wi-Fi 6E/7 for 2-5 Gig, and a 10 Gbps WAN port with Wi-Fi 7 for 8 Gig, plus Cat6 or better cable. Support covers the signal to the Fiber Jack only, not third-party router setup, and the gFiber App can't manage a third-party router.

Before you call Google Fiber

Google Fiber recommends these steps:

  1. Check for an outage (gFiber App, outage tool, or account portal); if there is one, no troubleshooting is needed.
  2. Check for account or payment alerts that could pause service.
  3. Run a gFiber speed test, ideally over a wired Ethernet connection.
  4. Temporarily disconnect from any VPN and retest.
  5. Update device software or test with a newer device.
  6. Restart the router (unplug 10 seconds, wait up to 5 minutes) or restart via the gFiber App.
  7. Place the router centrally, elevated, in the open; put mesh extenders halfway to the weak area.
  8. Check network health in the gFiber App.

When you do call, bring a record: when it was slow, for how long, and proof your router answered normally the whole time. That moves a ticket past “restart your router.”
