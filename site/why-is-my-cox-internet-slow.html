Why is my Cox internet slow?

Slow Cox internet has two possible sources: your home Wi-Fi, or Cox's network. Here's how to tell them apart, the slowdowns typical of Cox, and the fixes that work.

By Corey Haines, maker of WiFi or ISP · Updated October 6, 2026

The short answer

Compare your router with the internet. If the router is quick and the internet is slow, it's Cox. Check for an outage, restart your equipment in the right order, and if it keeps happening, log it and contact Cox with the evidence.

Charter completed its acquisition of Cox on August 20, 2026. Spectrum branding, pricing and packaging launched in Cox markets in mid-September 2026, and cox.com now shows 'Cox is now Spectrum'. Some Cox support pages, such as the speeds-and-data-plans disclosure, now redirect to or reuse Spectrum content.

First, rule out your Wi-Fi

A slow Cox connection and a slow Wi-Fi network feel identical. Time a round trip to your router and one to the internet at the same moment. If your router answers quickly and the internet doesn't, the slowdown is on Cox's side. If even your router is slow, it's your Wi-Fi or router, and Cox can't fix it. Here's how to check on a Mac.

What usually slows down Cox

Cox delivers internet over cable (DOCSIS, hybrid fiber-coax), fiber (some homes, via ONT). The usual causes:

  • Peak-time congestion on the shared neighborhood cable segment (common to DOCSIS cable networks; not specifically stated by Cox)
  • An older modem: Cox says slow speeds can come from an older modem and points you to its certified modem list
  • Loose coax or Ethernet cables, or an Ethernet cable older than Cat5e (Cox recommends at least Cat5e)
  • Many devices using the connection at once (Cox's first step is to turn devices off)
  • Testing over WiFi or through a separate router instead of wired to the modem (Cox says to test wired to the modem/gateway and not through a router)
  • Gateway placement: Cox recommends a central location about 3 feet or higher, not in a cabinet or closet
  • Low upload capacity on standard cable tiers relative to download

Check for a Cox outage

Cox outage page

This page is now branded 'Spectrum Outage Information'. You check status by entering your address or signing in. You can sign up for outage text alerts in MyAccount or the Cox App by Spectrum. Cox says there is a Spectrum outage map, and SmartHelp (after you sign in) shows details for your address, including an estimated restoration time. During an outage, Cox says not to reset your equipment.

Restart your Cox equipment

  1. In the Cox app: sign in, find your equipment under My Services, tap Reset Modem in the My Internet section, then tap Begin reset. The reboot can take up to ten minutes, and Cox Voice is briefly interrupted.
  2. Manual: turn off devices using the internet, then unplug the modem's power cord (and the router's, if you use one).
  3. Check that the coax cable is tight at the wall and the modem, and check any Ethernet cables.
  4. Plug the modem back in and allow up to ten minutes for it to come online, then plug the router back in.

Your Cox equipment

Panoramic Wifi Gateway, a modem and router in one (WiFi 5 or WiFi 6). Example: the Technicolor CGM4981, a DOCSIS 3.1 tri-band WiFi 6 Panoramic Wifi Gateway that supports up to the Go Beyond Fast 2 Gig tier.

Admin page: 192.168.0.1 (Panoramic Wifi Gateway Admin Tool). The username is admin, and the password is on the gateway's bottom label; if that doesn't work, try 'password'.

App: Cox App by Spectrum (account, SmartHelp, modem reset) and the Cox Panoramic Wifi app (WiFi management and whole-home speed test)

Bridge mode: Yes, on the Panoramic Wifi Gateway. Go to the Admin Tool at 192.168.0.1 > Gateway > At a Glance > Bridge Mode: Enable, confirm, and the gateway restarts. Then connect your router to any gateway Ethernet port. Caveats: you can't use Panoramic Wifi features or Pods, and Bridge Mode can't be turned on if Pods are activated. It isn't compatible with MoCA services such as IPTV or Cox Homelife, or with fiber-delivered service (homes with an ONT). To turn it off again, your device must be wired to the gateway by Ethernet.

Your own equipment: Allowed. You can buy a retail modem from Cox's compatible/certified modem list, but you'll also need your own router for WiFi. Cox now says device support is limited to equipment rented or bought from Spectrum, and you lose the Panoramic Wifi perks. Combination internet and telephone modems bought new or used from third-party retailers are not certified for the Cox network.

Before you call Cox

Cox recommends these steps:

  1. Make sure your modem isn't outdated (check Cox's certified cable modems).
  2. Turn off devices that are using the internet (computers, phones, tablets, game consoles).
  3. Unplug the modem's power cord, and the router's if you use one.
  4. Check that the coax cable is tight at the wall and the modem, and that Ethernet cables (Cat5e or better) go to the router's Ethernet/IN/WAN port.
  5. Plug the modem back in and allow up to ten minutes, then plug the router back in.
  6. Turn on one computer, clear the browser cache and cookies, and run a speed test. Panoramic Wifi users can test in the Panoramic Wifi app; others should test wired directly to the modem, not through a router, with no other devices online.
  7. If it's still slow, try other devices to see whether the problem is one device or the whole household, then contact support.

When you do call, bring a record: when it was slow, for how long, and proof your router answered normally the whole time. That moves a ticket past “restart your router.”
