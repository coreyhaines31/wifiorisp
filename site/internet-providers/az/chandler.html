Internet providers in Chandler, AZ

Chandler has 14 home internet providers in the FCC's latest data, 7 of them wired. Fiber reaches at least 20% of homes, less than Arizona as a whole (38%), led by Cox Communications at up to 2 Gbps. Cox Communications cable covers 75% of homes, with uploads up to 500 Mbps.

Upload speeds in Chandler

Cable plans here top out at 500 Mbps up, against 8 Gbps on fiber. If you work from home, back up to the cloud, or stream, the upload number matters more than the download. Our speed guide shows how much you need.

5G home and fixed wireless

AT&T, AirFiber WISP, Arizona Network LLC, GeoLinks, T-Mobile, Tristate Wi-Fi by Wi-Fiber, Verizon and WeLink Communications Inc offer wireless home internet in Chandler. It needs no installer and is often cheaper, but speed depends on the signal where you live and on how busy the tower is in the evening.

Before you switch

If your internet is slow now, make sure the provider is the problem. The free WiFi or ISP Mac app times your router and the internet at the same moment and tells you which side is slow, and the bufferbloat test shows whether calls will stutter when the connection is busy.

Questions

What is the best internet provider in Chandler, AZ?

For most homes, fiber from Cox Communications if it reaches your street: up to 2 Gbps down and 35 Mbps up, with steady lag. It covers 20% of homes, so check your address first.

Is fiber internet available in Chandler, AZ?

Yes. AT&T, CenturyLink, Cox Communications, FiberFirst, LLC, Google Fiber and Quantum Fiber report fiber in Chandler, reaching at least 20% of homes.

What is the fastest internet in Chandler, AZ?

Quantum Fiber advertises up to 8 Gbps down and 8 Gbps up (fiber), offered to 12% of homes.

How many internet providers are in Chandler, AZ?

14: 7 wired (fiber, cable, or DSL) and 8 5G or fixed wireless, plus satellite from Starlink, Viasat, and HughesNet, which reaches almost every home.

Know whether it’s the provider before you switch.

WiFi or ISP is a free Mac menu bar app. It times your router and the internet at the same moment, says which side is slow, and keeps a log you can send your ISP.
