Internet providers in Austin, TX

Austin has 20 home internet providers in the FCC's latest data, 11 of them wired. Fiber reaches at least 67% of homes, about the same as Texas as a whole (71%), led by Google Fiber at up to 8 Gbps. Spectrum cable covers 95% of homes, with uploads up to 1 Gbps.

Upload speeds in Austin

Cable plans here top out at 1 Gbps up, against 8 Gbps on fiber. If you work from home, back up to the cloud, or stream, the upload number matters more than the download. Our speed guide shows how much you need.

5G home and fixed wireless

AT&T, Nextlink, PCs for People, Rise Broadband, SOS Communications LLC, Spry Air Fiber, T-Mobile, VTX Communications, LLC, Verizon, Webpass, Inc. and Western Broadband offer wireless home internet in Austin. It needs no installer and is often cheaper, but speed depends on the signal where you live and on how busy the tower is in the evening.

Before you switch

If your internet is slow now, make sure the provider is the problem. The free WiFi or ISP Mac app times your router and the internet at the same moment and tells you which side is slow, and the bufferbloat test shows whether calls will stutter when the connection is busy.

Questions

What is the best internet provider in Austin, TX?

For most homes, fiber from Google Fiber if it reaches your street: up to 8 Gbps down and 8 Gbps up, with steady lag. It covers 67% of homes, so check your address first.

Is fiber internet available in Austin, TX?

Yes. AT&T, Astound Broadband, Centric Fiber, Frontier, Google Fiber, Hotwire Communications, Optimum and Spectrum report fiber in Austin, reaching at least 67% of homes.

What is the fastest internet in Austin, TX?

Google Fiber advertises up to 8 Gbps down and 8 Gbps up (fiber), offered to 67% of homes.

How many internet providers are in Austin, TX?

20: 11 wired (fiber, cable, or DSL) and 11 5G or fixed wireless, plus satellite from Starlink, Viasat, and HughesNet, which reaches almost every home.

Know whether it’s the provider before you switch.

WiFi or ISP is a free Mac menu bar app. It times your router and the internet at the same moment, says which side is slow, and keeps a log you can send your ISP.
