Wi-Fi site survey on a Mac: find the dead zones room by room

Wi-Fi that's fine by the router and useless in the back bedroom is a coverage problem, and you can't fix what you haven't measured. Here's how to measure every room with a Mac in a few minutes, and how to tell whether a change helped.

By Corey Haines, maker of WiFi or ISP · Updated November 23, 2026

The short answer

Carry your Mac to each room and write down the signal in dBm: Option-click the Wi-Fi icon, or let the free wifiorisp survey command keep the table for you. Below −75 dBm is weak, and below −85 dBm is a dead zone. Change one thing, measure again, and count a room as better only when its signal moved by 3 dB or more.

What a Wi-Fi site survey is

A site survey measures Wi-Fi where you use it, not where the router sits. Professional tools draw a heat map over a floor plan, which is right for an office with twenty access points. At home you need two answers: which rooms are weak, and whether the thing you just changed made them better.

A table with one row per room gives you both.

Survey your home in a few minutes

The wifiorisp command comes with WiFi or ISP, a free Mac app. Open the Terminal app (press Command-Space and type Terminal) and install it with brew install --cask coreyhaines31/tap/wifiorisp, or download the app and link the command.

  1. Carry the Mac to a room. Sit where you'd use it.
  2. Take a reading. In Terminal, run wifiorisp survey room Kitchen, with the room's name. It takes a few seconds.
  3. Do the same in every room. Include the one that's always bad.
  4. Print the table. Run wifiorisp survey.

An example of what you get:

Room          Signal                Link rate   Router
Living room   Excellent (-48 dBm)   1201 Mbps   Fast · 3 ms
Kitchen       Good (-61 dBm)        720 Mbps    Fast · 4 ms
Office        Good (-64 dBm)        576 Mbps    Fast · 5 ms
Guest room    Fair (-72 dBm)        287 Mbps    Fast · 9 ms
Bedroom       Weak (-79 dBm)        144 Mbps    OK · 18 ms

Weakest signal: Bedroom.

Not a Terminal person? Choose Survey Rooms… from the app's menu, type the room's name, and press Measure Here. It fills in the same table.

Without the app, hold Option and click the Wi-Fi icon in the menu bar, read RSSI under your network, and write it down for each room. It's the same number.

How to read the table

  • Signal is the one to judge by. Closer to zero is stronger: −55 dBm or better is excellent, down to −67 is good, down to −75 is fair, and below −75 is weak enough that calls drop and pages stall. Below −85 is a dead zone. The full dBm chart is here.
  • Link rate is the speed of the Wi-Fi link between your Mac and the router. It isn't your internet speed, and it falls as the signal does.
  • Router is how long the router takes to answer. A few milliseconds is normal. A slow answer in a room with a good signal points at a crowded channel or a struggling router, not at distance.

Fix the weak rooms

Change one thing at a time, so you know what worked.

  • Move the router up high and toward the middle of the home, out of cabinets and away from metal, mirrors, and fish tanks. It's free and often worth the most.
  • Change the channel when the signal is good but the router answers slowly. Here's how.
  • Add a mesh point halfway between the router and the dead zone, not inside the dead zone, where it would have nothing to repeat.
  • Replace an old router if every room is weak. Here's what to look for.

Check that it worked

Run wifiorisp survey again, then go back to each room and take the readings again. The table now shows before and after:

Room          Signal               Link rate          Router
Living room   -48 → -50 dBm (-2)   1201 → 1201 Mbps   3 → 3 ms
Kitchen       -61 → -58 dBm (+3)   720 → 864 Mbps     4 → 4 ms
Office        -64 → -60 dBm (+4)   576 → 720 Mbps     5 → 4 ms
Guest room    -72 → -66 dBm (+6)   287 → 432 Mbps     9 → 6 ms
Bedroom       -79 → -70 dBm (+9)   144 → 287 Mbps     18 → 8 ms

Stronger signal in 4 of 5 rooms.

Count a room as better only when its signal moved by 3 dB or more. Signal wobbles by a decibel or two with the Mac sitting still, so the living room's −2 above is noise, and 3 dB is double the power.

Don't score the change by link rate. It hops between fixed steps from second to second. On our test Mac, eight readings in the same spot bounced between 864 and 1152 Mbps while the signal stayed within 1 dB. A before and after built on link rate can show a big gain that never happened.

Questions

What is a Wi-Fi dead zone?

A spot where the signal is too weak to use. Below −75 dBm is weak enough that calls drop and pages stall, and below −85 dBm a device barely connects at all.

Do I need a heat map?

Not at home. A heat map helps when you're placing many access points across an office. For a house or apartment, one reading per room tells you where it's weak and whether a change helped.

How many readings do I need?

One per room you use, taken where you actually sit. Take them again after each change, in the same spots.

Is link rate my internet speed?

No. It's the speed of the Wi-Fi link between your device and the router. Your internet speed is set by your plan and your ISP, and it can be far lower than the link rate.

Can I survey with an iPhone?

Not with this command, which runs on a Mac. An iPhone only shows signal in dBm through Apple's free AirPort Utility app, with its Wi-Fi Scanner turned on in Settings, so a Mac laptop is the easier way to walk the rooms.

Know which rooms are weak, and when a fix worked.
