Why is my Frontier internet slow?

Slow Frontier internet has two possible sources: your home Wi-Fi, or Frontier's network. Here's how to tell them apart, the slowdowns typical of Frontier, and the fixes that work.

By Corey Haines, maker of WiFi or ISP · Updated October 6, 2026

The short answer

Compare your router with the internet. If the router is quick and the internet is slow, it's Frontier. Check for an outage, restart your equipment in the right order, and if it keeps happening, log it and contact Frontier with the evidence.

Verizon's acquisition of Frontier closed January 20, 2026. A Frontier wholesale bulletin says Frontier is now a wholly owned subsidiary of Verizon, integration is expected to continue through the end of 2026, and support contacts and products are unchanged for now. Fiber plans advertise no data caps. Frontier says its speed test measures 'speed to device', which Wi-Fi and device limits affect, and that maximum speeds are wired.

First, rule out your Wi-Fi

A slow Frontier connection and a slow Wi-Fi network feel identical. Time a round trip to your router and one to the internet at the same moment. If your router answers quickly and the internet doesn't, the slowdown is on Frontier's side. If even your router is slow, it's your Wi-Fi or router, and Frontier can't fix it. Here's how to check on a Mac.

What usually slows down Frontier

Frontier delivers internet over fiber (FTTH), DSL (copper). The usual causes:

  • DSL: landline phones without DSL filters. Phones should plug into the filter's 'Phone' port and the modem into the 'DSL' port (Frontier support wizard).
  • VPN software. Frontier suggests testing without the VPN and says that if the connection improves, the VPN is likely the problem.
  • Bandwidth-heavy devices (streaming movies, high-intensity gaming) and background updates on other devices.
  • Wi-Fi interference from other electronics, walls and doors, and nearby Wi-Fi networks on the same channel. Restarting the router makes it rescan for the least congested channel.
  • Distance from the router, and older devices or older Wi-Fi technology.
  • A plan with too little bandwidth for the number of users and devices.
  • Inside wiring at the premises and network congestion (Frontier internet disclosures).

Upload versus download

Fiber is symmetric: Fiber 500 (500/500), 1 Gig (1000/1000), 2 Gig (2000/2000), 5 Gig (5000/5000) and 7 Gig (7000/7000). DSL is asymmetric: Frontier says the most common DSL type gives download much more bandwidth than upload because most users download more than they upload. When an upload fills that smaller pipe, everything else lags, which is called bufferbloat. Test for it.

Check for a Frontier outage

Frontier outage page

The online checker requires sign-in (you enter your service address). No sign-in needed: text OUTAGE to 70994 and Frontier will text updates until the outage is resolved. Outages can also be checked under Support in the MyFrontier app.

Restart your Frontier equipment

  1. Router, gateway or modem: unplug the unit.
  2. Wait 30 seconds.
  3. Plug it back in and give it a few minutes to fully power up.
  4. Fiber ONT: if a battery backup unit is attached, remove one of the battery leads so it stops powering the ONT. Unplug the ONT, wait at least five minutes, then plug it back in, and make sure the ONT lights are on.

Your Frontier equipment

Fiber: Amazon eero routers. Fiber 500, 1 Gig and 2 Gig plans get the eero Pro 7; Fiber 5 Gig and 7 Gig get the eero Max 7 (both Wi-Fi 7). Fiber customers also have an ONT (Optical Network Terminal). Older and DSL routers named in Frontier docs: Arris NVG443B, NVG448B, NVG448BQ, NVG468MQ and NVG589; Netgear 7550 and D2200D; Actiontec F2250, GT784WNV and WR424; Greenwave G1100; Pace 4111N, 5031NV and 5168NV; D-Link DSL 2750B.

Admin page: eero: managed in the eero app (opened from the MyFrontier app), not via a browser IP. Other Frontier routers, per Frontier's help page: 192.168.254.254 (Arris NVG443B/448B/448BQ/468MQ, Netgear D2200D, Netgear 7550); 192.168.1.254 (Arris NVG589, Pace 4111N/5031NV/5168NV); 192.168.0.1 (Actiontec F2250, GT784WNV, WR424); 192.168.1.1 (D-Link DSL 2750B, Greenwave G1100).

App: MyFrontier app (eero Wi-Fi settings open in the eero app from inside MyFrontier)

Your own equipment: You must use the Frontier router or modem the first time you activate service. After that you may replace the Frontier router with one you buy, but Frontier says it 'only supports the routers we supply to you'. With a non-Frontier router, Frontier may not be able to manage some or all of your services, remote troubleshooting and support will be limited, and the router may not be compatible.

Before you call Frontier

Frontier recommends these steps:

  1. Reboot your equipment: unplug the unit, wait 30 seconds, plug it back in and give it a few minutes to fully power up.
  2. Check whether you are using a VPN, and test for a while without it.
  3. Pause bandwidth-heavy activity such as streaming movies or high-intensity gaming on other devices.
  4. DSL (non-Fiber) customers: make sure DSL filters are installed on landline phones, with phones on the 'Phone' port and the modem on the 'DSL' port.
  5. Run a speed test, preferably over a wired connection with VPNs, backups and streaming turned off.
  6. If problems continue, contact Frontier Technical Support.

When you do call, bring a record: when it was slow, for how long, and proof your router answered normally the whole time. That moves a ticket past “restart your router.”
