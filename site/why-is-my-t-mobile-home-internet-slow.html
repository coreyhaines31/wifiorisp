T-Mobile Home Internet

Why is my T-Mobile Home Internet slow?

Slow T-Mobile Home Internet has two possible sources: your home Wi-Fi, or T-Mobile Home Internet's network. Here's how to tell them apart, the slowdowns typical of T-Mobile Home Internet, and the fixes that work.

By Corey Haines, maker of WiFi or ISP · Updated October 6, 2026

The short answer

Compare your router with the internet. If the router is quick and the internet is slow, it's T-Mobile Home Internet. Check for an outage, restart your equipment in the right order, and if it keeps happening, log it and contact T-Mobile Home Internet with the evidence.

Home Internet support phone (from T-Mobile's site): 1-844-275-9310. T-Mobile's Network Traffic Prioritization & Management page (URL redirects to /responsibility/consumer-info/policies/internet-service/network-management-practices) says stationary Home Internet customers 'should be less likely to notice congestion in general.' T-Mobile has no official speed test page of its own; its troubleshooting page points to Speedtest.net. AWAY plan: speeds drop to 128kbps once the monthly data is used.

First, rule out your Wi-Fi

A slow T-Mobile Home Internet connection and a slow Wi-Fi network feel identical. Time a round trip to your router and one to the internet at the same moment. If your router answers quickly and the internet doesn't, the slowdown is on T-Mobile Home Internet's side. If even your router is slow, it's your Wi-Fi or router, and T-Mobile Home Internet can't fix it. Here's how to check on a Mac.

What usually slows down T-Mobile Home Internet

T-Mobile Home Internet delivers internet over 5G fixed wireless, 4G LTE fixed wireless. The usual causes:

  • Deprioritization: T-Mobile's network management disclosure says Home Internet customers generally get the same network priority as Mobile Wireless Heavy Data Users. Customers may notice slower speeds than higher-priority customers during congestion. Where the network is heavily loaded they will likely see significant drops in speed, especially for data-intensive activities.
  • Heavy use: since May 8, 2024, Internet customers who use more than 1.2TB in a billing cycle become Internet Heavy Data Users and are prioritized last on the network. The status resets at the next bill cycle.
  • The FAQ says that during congestion, Internet customers may see slower speeds than customers on other T-Mobile services, and slower still above 1.2TB/month, because of data prioritization.
  • Speeds vary with location, time of day, signal strength and availability (Home Internet FAQ). Weak signal at the gateway's location: use the T-Life app to check signal and find the best placement.
  • Home Wi-Fi interference: microwaves, baby monitors, Bluetooth devices, fluorescent lights and large metal appliances near the gateway; distance and walls.
  • Internet Lite plans: slowdowns once the data allowance is used up.

Upload versus download

Very asymmetric. T-Mobile's Home Internet FAQ: All-In or Amplified Home Internet typically 170-498 Mbps download and 12-55 Mbps upload. Rely Home Internet typically 170-354 Mbps download and 12-55 Mbps upload. 25% of customers see speeds below these ranges and 25% above. There are no speed tiers. When an upload fills that smaller pipe, everything else lags, which is called bufferbloat. Test for it.

Check for a T-Mobile Home Internet outage

T-Mobile Home Internet outage page

T-Mobile has no public outage map for Home Internet. Its Network Outages page explains outage notifications (tower maintenance or unplanned problems) and says T-Mobile will tell you when the outage is fixed, usually within two days. You may need to power cycle the gateway afterward. The T-Life app's diagnostics check the gateway's connection to the T-Mobile network.

Restart your T-Mobile Home Internet equipment

  1. Unplug the gateway from power, wait 30 seconds, then plug it back in. Allow 2 minutes for it to fully reconnect before testing (T-Mobile Troubleshooting page).
  2. Or in the T-Life app: go to the Overview screen, check that the gateway is online, select Restart your gateway, and wait for it to restart.
  3. Don't restart the gateway while it is updating. It restarts on its own when the update finishes (5G Gateway Gen5 page).

Your T-Mobile Home Internet equipment

5G Gateway Gen5 (G5AR, G5SE), 5G Gateway Gen4 (G4AR, G4SE). Older gateways: Sagemcom FAST 5688W, Arcadyan KVD21, Nokia 5G21, Askey LTE Wi-Fi Gateway.

Admin page: http://192.168.12.1 is documented only for older gateways (Arcadyan KVD21 and Nokia 5G21 support pages). The login is on the label on the underside of the gateway.

App: T-Life app

Bridge mode: Not supported. T-Mobile states that its gateways can't turn bridge mode on or off.

Your own equipment: A T-Mobile gateway is required (service runs through the SIM in the gateway). Third-party routers can connect to the gateway's Ethernet port. Both the gateway and the third-party router will broadcast Wi-Fi unless you turn off the gateway's Wi-Fi in the T-Life app. Mesh systems and switches can also be connected.

Before you call T-Mobile Home Internet

T-Mobile Home Internet recommends these steps:

  1. Run a network diagnostics test in the T-Life app (it checks the gateway's Wi-Fi and its connection to the T-Mobile network).
  2. Restart the gateway: unplug it, wait 30 seconds, plug it back in, and allow 2 minutes before testing.
  3. Check which Wi-Fi band your device uses. Create a 2.4GHz network for devices that are far away or older.
  4. Reduce interference near the gateway (microwaves, baby monitors, Bluetooth devices, fluorescent lights, large metal appliances).
  5. Move any mesh unit to a central, open, mid-height spot.
  6. On an Internet Lite plan, check your data usage.
  7. Use the T-Life app to check signal and find the best placement for the gateway.
  8. Move the device to within 15 feet of the gateway, and restart connected devices.
  9. Run a speed test at Speedtest.net to confirm the gateway is getting good network speeds.
  10. If it's still slow, contact T-Mobile.

When you do call, bring a record: when it was slow, for how long, and proof your router answered normally the whole time. That moves a ticket past “restart your router.”
