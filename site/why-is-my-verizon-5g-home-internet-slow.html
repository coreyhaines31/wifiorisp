Verizon 5G Home

Why is my Verizon 5G Home internet slow?

Slow Verizon 5G Home internet has two possible sources: your home Wi-Fi, or Verizon 5G Home's network. Here's how to tell them apart, the slowdowns typical of Verizon 5G Home, and the fixes that work.

By Corey Haines, maker of WiFi or ISP · Updated October 6, 2026

The short answer

Compare your router with the internet. If the router is quick and the internet is slow, it's Verizon 5G Home. Check for an outage, restart your equipment in the right order, and if it keeps happening, log it and contact Verizon 5G Home with the evidence.

Verizon's 5G Home marketing says 'no data limits', but the network management policy applies: lines over 1.83 TB/month (as of June 2026) may be deprioritized during congestion. Service may only be used at the registered service address. Verizon's general contact page is https://www.verizon.com/support/contact-us/ (it redirects to a contact app).

First, rule out your Wi-Fi

A slow Verizon 5G Home connection and a slow Wi-Fi network feel identical. Time a round trip to your router and one to the internet at the same moment. If your router answers quickly and the internet doesn't, the slowdown is on Verizon 5G Home's side. If even your router is slow, it's your Wi-Fi or router, and Verizon 5G Home can't fix it. Here's how to check on a Mac.

What usually slows down Verizon 5G Home

Verizon 5G Home delivers internet over 5G fixed wireless (5G Ultra Wideband / 5G), 4G LTE (LTE Home in some locations). The usual causes:

  • Weak 5G signal at the gateway's location. Verizon recommends a window or wall facing the tower, on a higher floor, away from cabinets and metal (Gateway 5G Optimizer article).
  • Network congestion. 5G Home and LTE Home lines that use more than the Heavy Data User threshold (1.83 TB in a billing month as of June 2026) may have their data prioritized behind other traffic during congestion for the rest of the cycle (Verizon Important Plan Information, Network Management).
  • Disconnections when the connection switches between cell towers, or when bad weather disrupts service (Verizon's 'What is slowing my internet down?' guide).
  • Uploads may fall back to 4G LTE when the signal is weaker (Important Plan Information).
  • Speeds vary with address/location, equipment, network connection and outside factors affecting cellular networks (Important Plan Information).
  • Home Wi-Fi problems: interference from Bluetooth devices, microwaves and baby monitors, too many connected devices, or a VPN (Verizon Wi-Fi slow-connection FAQs).

Upload versus download

Very asymmetric. From Verizon's Important Plan Information: 5G Home up to 100 Mbps: 25-85 Mbps typical download / 5-10 Mbps upload. 5G Home up to 300 Mbps: 85-300 down / 10-20 up. 5G Home Plus up to 500 Mbps: 200-500 down / 20-35 up. 5G Home Ultimate up to 1 Gig: 300-1000 down / 25-75 up. LTE Home: 25-50 down / 4-5 up. Uploads may go over 5G and 4G LTE when the signal is weaker. When an upload fills that smaller pipe, everything else lags, which is called bufferbloat. Test for it.

Check for a Verizon 5G Home outage

Verizon 5G Home outage page

Verizon's network status tool: select the Home Internet tab, enter your address or use your location, then Check now. If you sign in to My Verizon and Verizon knows of an outage in your area, a Network Notification alert shows at the top of the screen.

Restart your Verizon 5G Home equipment

  1. Restart the router from the My Verizon app or My Verizon online (Verizon's 5G Home support overview).
  2. Or unplug the router for 30 seconds, then plug it back in (Verizon's 'What is slowing my internet down?' guide).
  3. Restart your devices, and check that power and Ethernet cables are plugged in and undamaged.

Your Verizon 5G Home equipment

Verizon Internet Gateway (ARC-XCI55AX, ASK-NCQ1338, ASK-NCQ1338FA, ASK-NCM1100, WNC-CR200A), Verizon 5G Internet Gateway (LVSKIHP), Verizon 5G Home Router (LVSKR1), Verizon Receiver (LV65), Verizon Router (CR1000A/CR1000B). Verizon's support pages list these models; which one you get depends on your plan and area.

Admin page: mynetworksettings.com, or https://192.168.1.1 (WNC-CR200A and ARC-XCI55AX) / https://192.168.0.1 (ASK-NCQ1338 and ASK-NCQ1338FA), per Verizon's 'Verizon Internet Gateway - Access Admin Page' article. Browsers may show a certificate warning; the admin password is on the label on the bottom of the router.

App: Verizon Home app (speed test, Gateway 5G Optimizer); restarts can also be done from the My Verizon app

Bridge mode: Supported on the Verizon Internet Gateway as IP Passthrough: Admin page > Advanced > Network Settings > Network Connections > Network (Home/Office) > Settings > Bridge section > check IP Passthrough > Save. The router reboots (1-2 minutes). Your own router then plugs into the LAN2 Ethernet port and gets the network-assigned IP. All Wi-Fi radios turn off, and the internet no longer works over Wi-Fi or LAN1. To turn it off, connect a device to LAN1, sign in to the admin web UI and uncheck IP Passthrough.

Your own equipment: The Verizon Internet Gateway is required. Verizon owns it and you must return it within 30 days of cancelling or pay an unreturned equipment fee. Verizon's IP passthrough article says you can connect your own router, switch or other network gear behind the gateway through its LAN2 port.

Before you call Verizon 5G Home

Verizon 5G Home recommends these steps:

  1. Restart your router and modem: unplug them for 30 seconds and plug them back in.
  2. Restart your devices.
  3. Check all cables and connections.
  4. Run a speed test at Verizon.com (or in the Verizon Home app). If speeds are low, repeat steps 1-3.
  5. Check Verizon's network status tool for an outage at your address.
  6. Use the Gateway 5G Optimizer in the Verizon Home app to find the spot with the strongest 5G signal.
  7. If there's no outage and it's still not working, contact Verizon Support.

When you do call, bring a record: when it was slow, for how long, and proof your router answered normally the whole time. That moves a ticket past “restart your router.”
