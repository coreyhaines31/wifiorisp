Router login

Google Nest Wifi router login

How to get into your Google Nest Wifi / Google Wifi settings, and the three settings that fix most slow Wi-Fi: the channel, a restart, and smart queueing.

By Corey Haines, maker of WiFi or ISP · Updated October 6, 2026

How to log in

Google Nest Wifi has no web admin page. You manage it in the Google Home app.

Nest Wifi Pro, Nest Wifi and Google Wifi are set up and managed only in the Google Home app (the old Google Wifi app is retired). There is no browser admin page. The router's LAN address defaults to the 192.168.86.x range (DHCP pool 192.168.86.20-250) and can change to 192.168.85.x behind another Google Wifi network.

Username and password

No router password; you sign in to the Google Home app with your Google Account.

Change the Wi-Fi channel

  1. Not user-changeable. Google says the router and points work together to keep devices on the clearest channel automatically.

Not sure which channel? Here's how to pick a quiet one.

Restart it

  1. Whole network: Google Home app > Home > Wifi > Network settings > Restart entire network.
  2. One point: Google Home app > Home > All devices, touch and hold the device tile > Settings > Restart Wifi point.

Smart queueing (fixes lag under load)

Preferred activities and Prioritize device. Preferred activities (Home > Wifi > Network settings > Preferred activities) focuses bandwidth on video conferencing. Prioritize device reserves more bandwidth for one device for a set time. Neither is described as a bufferbloat/SQM feature.

Test for bufferbloat before and after turning it on.

Firmware updates

Automatic. Google rolls out updates over days or weeks; the device needs to stay online.

Find your router's address on a Mac

  1. Open Apple menu > System Settings, then click Wi-Fi in the sidebar.
  2. Click Details next to the network you're connected to.
  3. Click TCP/IP. The address next to Router is your router's address.
  4. Shortcut: Option-click the Wi-Fi icon in the menu bar; the router address is listed under your network.

See if your router is the slow part.

WiFi or ISP is a free Mac menu bar app. It times your router and the internet at the same moment, says which side is slow, and keeps a log you can send your ISP.
