Router login

Verizon Fios router login

How to get into your Verizon Fios Router settings, and the three settings that fix most slow Wi-Fi: the channel, a restart, and smart queueing.

By Corey Haines, maker of WiFi or ISP · Updated October 6, 2026

How to log in

  1. Connect to its network over Wi-Fi or a cable.
  2. Open a browser and go to mynetworksettings.com, myfiosgateway.com, 192.168.1.1.
  3. Sign in with the admin password (see below).

Basic settings are in My Verizon. The full router interface is at mynetworksettings.com (Verizon Router CR1000A) or myfiosgateway.com (Fios Router pages); 192.168.1.1 works for both. Expect a browser 'connection is not private' warning; Verizon's guide says to proceed to 192.168.1.1.

Username and password

Username admin. Verizon says the unique default password (the 'Network Settings password' / admin password) is printed on the label on the back of the router. The same label lists the default Wi-Fi name, Wi-Fi password and local URL.

Change the Wi-Fi channel

  1. Verizon Router (CR1000A): sign in at mynetworksettings.com.
  2. From the Advanced menu, select Wi-Fi, then Radio Management.
  3. Optionally click Scan to rate channels (Channel Analysis shows a 0-10 congestion score).
  4. Under Channel Settings, pick a channel or leave it on Auto.
  5. To keep a manual channel after a reboot, open Settings on that page and tick 'Keep my channel selection during power cycle', then Apply changes.

Restart it

  1. In the router interface: System > Reboot Router > Reboot Device (Verizon warns to do this only when needed).
  2. Or press and hold the rear Reset button for at least three seconds for a soft reboot. Holding it ten seconds or more is a factory reset.

Smart queueing

The CR1000A guide lists WMM Wi-Fi QoS and Layer 2/3 QoS support in its specs, but no consumer smart-queue / bufferbloat setting.

Firmware updates

Over the air. The CR1000A guide lists a 'Firmware update (FOTA)' light pattern; no manual update path is described.

Find your router's address on a Mac

  1. Open Apple menu > System Settings, then click Wi-Fi in the sidebar.
  2. Click Details next to the network you're connected to.
  3. Click TCP/IP. The address next to Router is your router's address.
  4. Shortcut: Option-click the Wi-Fi icon in the menu bar; the router address is listed under your network.
