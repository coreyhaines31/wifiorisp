Router login

192.168.1.1

192.168.1.1 is a private address many routers use for their settings page. Here's how to log in, which routers use it, and what to do if it doesn't load.

By Corey Haines, maker of WiFi or ISP · Updated October 6, 2026

How to log in at 192.168.1.1

  1. Connect to your router's network over Wi-Fi or a cable. The address only works from inside your network.
  2. Type http://192.168.1.1 into your browser's address bar (not the search box).
  3. Sign in with the admin username and password, usually printed on a sticker on the router.

192.168.1.1 is a private address: it only reaches the router on the network you're connected to. If it doesn't load, your router probably uses a different address; look it up with the steps below. Some routers use HTTPS with a self-signed certificate, so a browser privacy warning is expected.

Routers that use 192.168.1.1

  • NETGEAR: documented alongside routerlogin.net.
  • Verizon Fios / Verizon Router: documented alongside mynetworksettings.com and myfiosgateway.com.
  • Linksys: documented as https://192.168.1.1 alongside myrouter.local.
  • TP-Link: suggested as a fallback if tplinkwifi.net and 192.168.0.1 don't load.

Brand guides:

  • NETGEAR (Nighthawk and Orbi)
  • Verizon Fios Router
  • Linksys (including Velop)
  • Ubiquiti UniFi (Dream Router, UniFi Express)
  • TP-Link (Archer and Deco)

Not loading? Find your router's real address

On a Mac

  1. Open Apple menu > System Settings, then click Wi-Fi in the sidebar.
  2. Click Details next to the network you're connected to.
  3. Click TCP/IP. The address next to Router is your router's address.
  4. Shortcut: Option-click the Wi-Fi icon in the menu bar; the router address is listed under your network.

On Windows

  1. Open Command Prompt (search for cmd).
  2. Type ipconfig and press Enter.
  3. Under your Wi-Fi or Ethernet adapter, the Default Gateway is your router's address.

On an iPhone

  1. Open Settings and tap Wi-Fi.
  2. Tap the info (i) button next to your network.
  3. Scroll to the Router field; that's your router's address.
