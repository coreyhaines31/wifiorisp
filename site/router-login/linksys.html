Router login

Linksys router login

How to get into your Linksys (including Velop) settings, and the three settings that fix most slow Wi-Fi: the channel, a restart, and smart queueing.

By Corey Haines, maker of WiFi or ISP · Updated October 6, 2026

How to log in

  1. Connect to its network over Wi-Fi or a cable.
  2. Open a browser and go to myrouter.local, 192.168.1.1.
  3. Sign in with the admin password (see below).

Go to myrouter.local or https://192.168.1.1 from a computer on the network (Linksys says a desktop computer is needed for the mesh web interface). You may have to click past a mobile-blocking page and a browser security warning. The Linksys app is the alternative.

Username and password

Enter your router password in the 'Access Velop/Router' field. If it's your first login or you've forgotten it, use Reset Password, which asks for the Recovery Key printed on the bottom of the router connected to your modem.

Change the Wi-Fi channel

  1. Web: sign in, click Wi-Fi Settings, and set Channel (and Channel width) there.
  2. App: menu > Wi-Fi Settings > Advanced Wi-Fi Settings > Channel Finder to scan for the best channels (some models re-scan automatically every 5 days).

Settings vary by model.

Not sure which channel? Here's how to pick a quiet one.

Restart it

  1. Linksys app: Network Administration > Restart Network.

Smart queueing (fixes lag under load)

Media Prioritization / Priority (QoS). Smart Wi-Fi routers have Media Prioritization; some other Linksys routers put it under Applications & Gaming > QoS; Velop's Priority feature lets you pick up to three devices for bandwidth first. Not an SQM/bufferbloat feature.

Test for bufferbloat before and after turning it on.

Firmware updates

Mesh systems update automatically by default (web interface: Router Settings > Connectivity, 'Automatic' box). On Smart Wi-Fi routers automatic updates are off by default and can be enabled in the Firmware Update section.

Find your router's address on a Mac

  1. Open Apple menu > System Settings, then click Wi-Fi in the sidebar.
  2. Click Details next to the network you're connected to.
  3. Click TCP/IP. The address next to Router is your router's address.
  4. Shortcut: Option-click the Wi-Fi icon in the menu bar; the router address is listed under your network.

See if your router is the slow part.

WiFi or ISP is a free Mac menu bar app. It times your router and the internet at the same moment, says which side is slow, and keeps a log you can send your ISP.
