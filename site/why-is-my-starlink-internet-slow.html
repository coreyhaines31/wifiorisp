Why is my Starlink internet slow?

Slow Starlink internet has two possible sources: your home Wi-Fi, or Starlink's network. Here's how to tell them apart, the slowdowns typical of Starlink, and the fixes that work.

By Corey Haines, maker of WiFi or ISP · Updated October 6, 2026

The short answer

Compare your router with the internet. If the router is quick and the internet is slow, it's Starlink. Check for an outage, restart your equipment in the right order, and if it keeps happening, log it and contact Starlink with the evidence.

Peak hours are typically 6pm-11pm local time. The Residential plans include unlimited data, but Residential Lite data is always deprioritized. The Starlink router doesn't support MAC filtering or port forwarding.

First, rule out your Wi-Fi

A slow Starlink connection and a slow Wi-Fi network feel identical. Time a round trip to your router and one to the internet at the same moment. If your router answers quickly and the internet doesn't, the slowdown is on Starlink's side. If even your router is slow, it's your Wi-Fi or router, and Starlink can't fix it. Here's how to check on a Mac.

What usually slows down Starlink

Starlink delivers internet over low-Earth-orbit satellite. The usual causes:

  • Obstructions: trees, buildings, new leaves in spring, debris or snow on the dish. These cause interruptions that are most noticeable on video calls and gaming (app alerts and 'How to fix obstructions').
  • Peak-hour congestion, typically 6pm-11pm local time, when speeds may vary or be slower (Starlink 'Slow Speeds - Outside/During Peak Hours').
  • Plan-based deprioritization: Residential Lite data is 'always deprioritized' compared with other fixed plans, so it is slower in congested areas and at peak hours. Residential 100/200 Mbps and Roam customers are more likely to slow down during congestion than Residential Max. Priority data takes precedence over Residential and Roam (Service Plan Descriptions).
  • Network management: if your bandwidth use consistently exceeds a typical residential user's, Starlink may reduce your speeds (Service Plan Descriptions / Fair Use Policy).
  • Priority plans that run out of priority data drop to up to 1 Mbps download and 0.5 Mbps upload.
  • Elevated packet loss from extreme weather, temporary network issues or obstructions (RECENT INTERRUPTIONS alert).
  • Thermal throttling when the dish or power supply runs hot.
  • Using Starlink away from its registered service location, or on Mobile/Roam service.
  • Local problems: a VPN, the 2.4 GHz band or distance from the router, Ethernet cable below Cat5e, and third-party routers, mesh or switches.

Upload versus download

Very asymmetric. Starlink's support/specs search results show typical downloads of roughly 45-280 Mbps and uploads of 10-30 Mbps. Residential 100 Mbps and 200 Mbps plans cap download at the number in the plan name (Service Plan Descriptions). When an upload fills that smaller pipe, everything else lags, which is called bufferbloat. Test for it.

Check for a Starlink outage

Starlink has no public outage status page. Outages show in the Starlink app as a SERVICE OUTAGE alert ('Your area is currently experiencing a service outage'). The app home screen also shows alerts for obstructions and hardware problems, and the Statistics page shows outages. US residential customers who are offline can call 1-866-606-5103 (a 24/7 trial phone line).

Restart your Starlink equipment

  1. Reboot from the Starlink app (Reboot is one of the router settings in the app).
  2. Or power cycle Starlink and the router by unplugging the AC cable from the wall outlet and plugging it back in (Starlink's 'Slow Speeds - Outside/During Peak Hours' article).

Your Starlink equipment

Starlink Router 3 (included with the Standard/V4 kit), Router Mini, Gen 2 Router, Gen 1 Router. The dish connects to the router by the Starlink cable.

Admin page: The Starlink Router has no administrator portal. All router settings are in the Starlink app (per Starlink's 'Does Starlink have a WiFi router administrator portal?' article). The Starlink device dashboard is at http://192.168.100.1. With a third-party router you need a static route (192.168.100.0/24 via WAN) to reach it.

App: Starlink app (iOS/Android): speed tests including Advanced Speed Test, alerts, obstruction checks, Wi-Fi settings, reboot

Bridge mode: Yes. Router 3 and Router Mini support Bypass Mode: Starlink app > Settings > Bypass Mode > Enable. This turns off the Starlink router's Wi-Fi so a third-party router plugged into its Ethernet port runs the network. A violet light shows for 1 hour. The only way to exit bypass mode is a factory reset. Kits with a power supply that has built-in Ethernet (Performance Gen 1/Gen 2, Enterprise) don't need bypass; plug the router into the power supply. On Gen 1 Router kits, replace the Starlink router.

Your own equipment: The Starlink dish is required. Most kits support a third-party router over Ethernet (Standard: Ethernet built into the power supply; Standard Actuated needs the Starlink Ethernet Adapter plus Bypass Mode; Mini uses the USB-C to Ethernet adapter). Set the router's WAN to DHCP. Starlink offers only limited troubleshooting for non-Starlink equipment and suggests removing third-party gear when testing speeds.

Before you call Starlink

Starlink recommends these steps:

  1. Turn off any VPN and run a speed test again.
  2. Check whether all devices are slow or just one. For a single Wi-Fi device, try the 5 GHz band close to the router, or split the 2.4/5 GHz networks in the app and reboot the router. For Ethernet, use Cat5e cable or better.
  3. Use the Advanced Speed Test in the Starlink app (Router to Internet, Device to Router, Device to Internet), on 5 GHz and close to the router.
  4. With a third-party router, connect a computer by Ethernet directly to the Starlink power supply, or bypass your router, to test.
  5. Remove third-party routers, mesh and switches, and test with only Starlink equipment.
  6. Compare your results with the Starlink availability map. Within about 10 Mbps of the listed speed is normal.
  7. Check the app home screen for outage, obstruction or hardware alerts. Make sure Starlink is at the service location on your account.
  8. Make sure nothing is covering or blocking the dish (dirt, debris). Use 'Check for Obstructions' and relocate if needed.
  9. Power cycle Starlink and the router.
  10. If speeds are still slow, submit a support ticket through the Starlink app.

When you do call, bring a record: when it was slow, for how long, and proof your router answered normally the whole time. That moves a ticket past “restart your router.”
