Why is my AT&T internet slow?

Slow AT&T internet has two possible sources: your home Wi-Fi, or AT&T's network. Here's how to tell them apart, the slowdowns typical of AT&T, and the fixes that work.

By Corey Haines, maker of WiFi or ISP · Updated October 6, 2026

The short answer

Compare your router with the internet. If the router is quick and the internet is slow, it's AT&T. Check for an outage, restart your equipment in the right order, and if it keeps happening, log it and contact AT&T with the evidence.

AT&T no longer offers DSL service to new customers (att.com/internet/dsl). Data allowances: AT&T Internet tiers 768Kbps-75Mbps get 1.5TB/month; Internet 100 and higher (incl. AT&T Fiber) are unlimited; legacy DSL gets 150GB/month; overage is $10 per 50GB, capped at $100/month for AT&T Internet and $200 for DSL.

First, rule out your Wi-Fi

A slow AT&T connection and a slow Wi-Fi network feel identical. Time a round trip to your router and one to the internet at the same moment. If your router answers quickly and the internet doesn't, the slowdown is on AT&T's side. If even your router is slow, it's your Wi-Fi or router, and AT&T can't fix it. Here's how to check on a Mac.

What usually slows down AT&T

AT&T delivers internet over fiber (AT&T Fiber, FTTH), copper / IPBB (AT&T Internet, fiber-to-the-node over phone lines), DSL (legacy copper, no longer sold). The usual causes:

  • Copper (AT&T Internet/DSL): speed is impacted by the distance between your home and the central office (AT&T 'Understand Internet Speeds').
  • Age of the wiring inside your building (copper).
  • On copper, TV and internet sharing the same line reduces the bandwidth available for internet.
  • Wi-Fi is slower than wired Ethernet, and Wi-Fi speed is shared among all devices connected to the gateway.
  • Distance from the gateway and obstructions (walls, doors, floors, concrete or rebar), plus interference from microwaves, baby monitors and other Wi-Fi networks.
  • Older devices, and devices with more than one radio like phones and tablets, may not reach faster speeds.
  • Many simultaneous activities: multiple devices, streaming, gaming, video conferencing, smart home devices.
  • Website server capacity and routing outside AT&T's network.

Upload versus download

Differs by connection type. AT&T Fiber: all tiers are symmetrical ("equal upload and download speeds"); tiers are 300 Mbps, 500 Mbps, up to 1 GIG, 2 GIG and 5 GIG. AT&T Internet over copper (IPBB) and DSL: asymmetrical, with upload far below download (e.g. IPBB Internet 100 is advertised as 100 Mbps down / 20 Mbps up per AT&T's broadband performance disclosure); AT&T describes DSL as suitable only for light use such as browsing or email. When an upload fills that smaller pipe, everything else lags, which is called bufferbloat. Test for it.

Check for a AT&T outage

AT&T outage page

The outage page requires sign-in (or a one-time code sent to a phone number on the account) to see outages for your address or account. AT&T also points customers to the Smart Home Manager app/online tool to check Wi-Fi issues and outages.

Restart your AT&T equipment

  1. Unplug the power cord from the back of the gateway/All-Fi Hub and the wall (remove the internal battery backup if it has one).
  2. Wait 20 seconds, then reinsert the battery if applicable.
  3. Plug the power cord back in and wait for the gateway to restart; it can take up to five minutes for it and your devices to come back online.
  4. Alternative: in the Smart Home Manager app, select Network (Wi-Fi icon) > Restart Wi-Fi and confirm.
  5. Do not confuse with Reset: holding the red Reset button for 15+ seconds restores factory defaults and erases custom Wi-Fi names and passwords.

Your AT&T equipment

AT&T-provided Wi-Fi gateway. AT&T lists common models as BGW320, BGW210, Pace 5268, NVG599 and NVG589; the BGW320 is branded the 'All-Fi Hub' (Wi-Fi 6, used on AT&T Fiber multi-gig plans) and the BGW620 is also supported by Smart Home Manager. Legacy DSL customers may have an AT&T-compatible DSL modem instead.

Admin page: 192.168.1.254 (AT&T: enter http://192.168.1.254 in a browser to reach the gateway user interface on any AT&T Wi-Fi gateway)

App: AT&T Smart Home Manager app (also usable online from a computer)

Bridge mode: AT&T Fiber/AT&T Internet gateways do not offer a documented true bridge mode for residential service; instead they support IP Passthrough. AT&T's residential steps for BGW210/BGW320/Pace 5268: go to http://192.168.1.254 > Firewall > Advanced (enter the Device Access Code), turn off the advanced firewall options, then IP Passthrough with Allocation Mode = Passthrough and Passthrough Mode = DHCPS-fixed, enter the MAC address of your router, save and restart. AT&T's business documentation notes only one device can be in passthrough, it loses the AT&T gateway's firewall protection, and it gets the WAN IP. Legacy DSL modems (e.g. Westell) can be set to true bridge mode with the router doing PPPoE.

Your own equipment: For AT&T Fiber and AT&T Internet, the AT&T gateway is AT&T-provided equipment ("We provide the gateway when you set up internet at your home") and must be returned on cancellation; you cannot replace it, but you can put your own router behind it (ideally via IP Passthrough). For legacy AT&T DSL, AT&T documents connecting a non-AT&T router to the DSL modem using PPPoE/bridge mode. AT&T Tech Support only supports routers from AT&T.

Before you call AT&T

AT&T recommends these steps:

  1. Check for an outage at att.com/outages (sign-in required).
  2. Check the status lights on your Wi-Fi gateway or All-Fi Hub.
  3. Restart the gateway (unplug for 20 seconds, wait up to 5 minutes).
  4. Run the AT&T speed test on your device and on the gateway (att.com/support/speedtest).
  5. Reduce the number of devices and heavy activities (streaming, gaming, video calls) running at once.
  6. Move closer to the gateway or remove obstructions and interference sources; use a wired connection where possible.
  7. Consider newer devices, since older devices may not reach faster speeds.

When you do call, bring a record: when it was slow, for how long, and proof your router answered normally the whole time. That moves a ticket past “restart your router.”
