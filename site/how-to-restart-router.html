How to restart your router (the right way)

Restarting fixes more internet problems than anything else, but the order matters when you have a separate modem and router. Here's how to do it properly, and what to do when it doesn't help.

By Corey Haines, maker of WiFi or ISP · Updated October 6, 2026

The short answer

Unplug the modem and the router. Wait a minute. Plug in the modem first and wait two minutes until its lights settle, then plug in the router. If you have a single gateway box, just unplug it for a minute.

The order that works

  1. Unplug both the modem (the box your ISP's cable, fiber, or phone line goes into) and the Wi-Fi router. If it's one combined gateway, unplug that.
  2. Wait at least 60 seconds. It lets the modem drop its session with your ISP so it starts fresh.
  3. Plug in the modem first. Wait about two minutes, until its online or internet light is steady.
  4. Then plug in the router. Give it another two minutes to start Wi-Fi.
  5. Check. Load a few different websites. Better: check that your router and the internet both answer quickly.

Fiber users: don't touch the fiber cable or the back of the fiber terminal. Restart from power only.

Restart from your phone instead

Most ISPs let you restart their equipment from an app or your online account, which also runs their own line checks. Steps from each company's documentation:

Spectrum

  1. Online: sign in at Spectrum.net, open the Services tab and then the Internet sub-tab, choose your equipment and select Restart Equipment (you can also do this in the My Spectrum App).
  2. Manual (coax customers only): unplug the power cord from the modem and from the WiFi router.
  3. Wait at least one minute, then reinsert any batteries and plug the modem back in.
  4. Wait at least two more minutes while the modem powers on. Its lights should gradually come on.
  5. Plug the WiFi router back in and give it at least two minutes to finish starting up.
  6. Open two or three different web pages to make sure the connection works.
  7. Fiber customers: Spectrum says not to touch the back of the fiber modem, because that can damage the fiber cable.

Xfinity

  1. In the Xfinity app (rented gateways): open the WiFi section, tap Troubleshoot, then select Restart your Gateway.
  2. Or run the same troubleshooting from Xfinity Assistant or xfinity.com on a computer.
  3. Manual: unplug the gateway's power cable, wait one minute and plug it back in.
  4. The restart can take up to 12 minutes, and your home network won't work in the meantime. With Xfinity Voice, you can't make or receive calls (including 911) until the gateway is back online.

AT&T

  1. Unplug the power cord from the back of the gateway/All-Fi Hub and the wall (remove the internal battery backup if it has one).
  2. Wait 20 seconds, then reinsert the battery if applicable.
  3. Plug the power cord back in and wait for the gateway to restart; it can take up to five minutes for it and your devices to come back online.
  4. Alternative: in the Smart Home Manager app, select Network (Wi-Fi icon) > Restart Wi-Fi and confirm.
  5. Do not confuse with Reset: holding the red Reset button for 15+ seconds restores factory defaults and erases custom Wi-Fi names and passwords.

Verizon Fios

  1. Unplug the router.
  2. Wait 1 minute.
  3. Plug the router back in.
  4. Wait 1-3 minutes for initialization to complete, then try connecting again.
  5. Alternative (Verizon Router CR1000A/B): press and hold the Reset button on the back for 3-5 seconds and wait for the Internet light to turn steady white. Holding it for 15 seconds is a factory reset.
  6. Alternative: restart the router from the Verizon Home app.

Verizon 5G Home

  1. Restart the router from the My Verizon app or My Verizon online (Verizon's 5G Home support overview).
  2. Or unplug the router for 30 seconds, then plug it back in (Verizon's 'What is slowing my internet down?' guide).
  3. Restart your devices, and check that power and Ethernet cables are plugged in and undamaged.

T-Mobile Home Internet

  1. Unplug the gateway from power, wait 30 seconds, then plug it back in. Allow 2 minutes for it to fully reconnect before testing (T-Mobile Troubleshooting page).
  2. Or in the T-Life app: go to the Overview screen, check that the gateway is online, select Restart your gateway, and wait for it to restart.
  3. Don't restart the gateway while it is updating. It restarts on its own when the update finishes (5G Gateway Gen5 page).

Cox

  1. In the Cox app: sign in, find your equipment under My Services, tap Reset Modem in the My Internet section, then tap Begin reset. The reboot can take up to ten minutes, and Cox Voice is briefly interrupted.
  2. Manual: turn off devices using the internet, then unplug the modem's power cord (and the router's, if you use one).
  3. Check that the coax cable is tight at the wall and the modem, and check any Ethernet cables.
  4. Plug the modem back in and allow up to ten minutes for it to come online, then plug the router back in.

Optimum

  1. In the My Optimum app: sign in with your Optimum ID, tap WiFi > More options > Manage router settings, then tap Restart my Internet equipment. The restart takes about 5 minutes.
  2. Or chat with Allie, Optimum's 24/7 virtual assistant, to have your equipment restarted remotely (about 5 minutes).
  3. Manually: unplug the power cord from your Gateway or modem (remove the battery backup if you have one, and unplug your router if you have one).
  4. Wait 30 seconds.
  5. Plug the power cord back in (reinsert the battery backup and plug the router back in if applicable).
  6. Try to access the Internet as you normally do.
  7. With an Optimum One or Optimum TV box: Home > Settings > System > INTERNET/WIFI AND PHONE > REBOOT, or hold the diamond button on the box's front panel for 10 seconds until 'GW Reset' appears.

Frontier

  1. Router, gateway or modem: unplug the unit.
  2. Wait 30 seconds.
  3. Plug it back in and give it a few minutes to fully power up.
  4. Fiber ONT: if a battery backup unit is attached, remove one of the battery leads so it stops powering the ONT. Unplug the ONT, wait at least five minutes, then plug it back in, and make sure the ONT lights are on.

CenturyLink

  1. Unplug the power cord from the back of the modem or from the wall outlet, and check that all the modem lights go off.
  2. Wait 30 seconds to 1 minute (at least 30 seconds so the modem memory fully clears).
  3. Plug the power cord back in and wait for the power and internet lights to turn green (or the single status light on the C4000). A full boot can take 3-5 minutes.
  4. Alternatively, in the My CenturyLink app go to Services and tap the restart modem button (top tasks area or the Equipment section). On the web, go to My CenturyLink > Services > Equipment > your modem > Restart Modem.
  5. Fiber with Premium WiFi: unplug the SmartNID power cord, wait at least 30 seconds, plug it back in and wait 2-3 minutes. Do not remove the fiber cords from the SmartNID.

Google Fiber

  1. Unplug the power cord.
  2. Wait at least 10 seconds.
  3. Plug the power cord back in.
  4. Wait up to 5 minutes for the device to turn back on (Wi-Fi 6E/Wi-Fi 7 router light goes solid green; Wi-Fi 6 router solid blue).
  5. Alternative: tap Restart router in the gFiber App and confirm.

Starlink

  1. Reboot from the Starlink app (Reboot is one of the router settings in the app).
  2. Or power cycle Starlink and the router by unplugging the AC cable from the wall outlet and plugging it back in (Starlink's 'Slow Speeds - Outside/During Peak Hours' article).

When a restart doesn't fix it

  • Slow again within days: the router may be overheating, out of date, or overloaded. Update its firmware, give it airflow, or replace it if it's more than five or six years old.
  • Still slow right after a restart: find out which side is slow. If your router answers quickly and the internet doesn't, the problem is past your home, and only your ISP can fix it. Here's how to tell.
  • The internet keeps dropping: log the drops, so your ISP can see when and how long.

Questions

How long should I unplug my router?

At least 60 seconds. Shorter restarts may not clear the modem's connection with your ISP.

Is restarting the same as resetting?

No. Restarting just turns it off and on. Resetting (usually holding a recessed button for several seconds) erases your Wi-Fi name, password, and settings. Don't reset unless support tells you to.

How often should I restart my router?

Only when something's wrong. If you need to restart it every few days, that's a sign of a deeper problem worth fixing.

Check that the restart actually worked.
