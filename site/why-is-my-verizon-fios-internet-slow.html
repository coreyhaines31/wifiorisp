Why is my Verizon Fios internet slow?

Slow Verizon Fios internet has two possible sources: your home Wi-Fi, or Verizon Fios's network. Here's how to tell them apart, the slowdowns typical of Verizon Fios, and the fixes that work.

By Corey Haines, maker of WiFi or ISP · Updated October 6, 2026

The short answer

Compare your router with the internet. If the router is quick and the internet is slow, it's Verizon Fios. Check for an outage, restart your equipment in the right order, and if it keeps happening, log it and contact Verizon Fios with the evidence.

Verizon's community forum links now redirect to the main support site (community sunset). The 'My Fios' app was folded into My Verizon on Nov 29, 2023; router control moved to the separate Verizon Home app.

First, rule out your Wi-Fi

A slow Verizon Fios connection and a slow Wi-Fi network feel identical. Time a round trip to your router and one to the internet at the same moment. If your router answers quickly and the internet doesn't, the slowdown is on Verizon Fios's side. If even your router is slow, it's your Wi-Fi or router, and Verizon Fios can't fix it. Here's how to check on a Mac.

What usually slows down Verizon Fios

Verizon Fios delivers internet over fiber (FTTH, Fios). The usual causes:

  • Wi-Fi vs. wired: Verizon notes a Wi-Fi speed test measures your Wi-Fi network, not the broadband speed delivered to the home.
  • 2.4 GHz band has greater range but lower max speed (Verizon cites 75 Mbps); 5 GHz is faster with less range.
  • Interference from other Wi-Fi networks, Bluetooth, cordless phones, microwaves, baby monitors, mirrors, and physical obstructions.
  • WEP security can reduce speed by up to 80%; use WPA2.
  • VPNs can slow your connection.
  • Multiple devices streaming at once and many devices sharing the connection.
  • Device limits: processor, memory, OS; gigabit speeds require a capable wired device.
  • Quality and condition of the wiring connecting your computers to the Verizon network.
  • Network outages or slow website servers.

Upload versus download

Nearly symmetrical. Verizon says Fios provides 'nearly symmetrical upload and download speeds'; plans are 300 Mbps, 500 Mbps, 1 Gig, 2 Gig and 5 Gig. Fios Gigabit is quoted at average wired download speeds of 750-940 Mbps with comparable upload. When an upload fills that smaller pipe, everything else lags, which is called bufferbloat. Test for it.

Check for a Verizon Fios outage

Verizon Fios outage page

Verizon's outage information page routes customers to Service Central, which requires signing in to My Verizon; outages for your address appear as a network notification there. The My Verizon app is also offered.

Restart your Verizon Fios equipment

  1. Unplug the router.
  2. Wait 1 minute.
  3. Plug the router back in.
  4. Wait 1-3 minutes for initialization to complete, then try connecting again.
  5. Alternative (Verizon Router CR1000A/B): press and hold the Reset button on the back for 3-5 seconds and wait for the Internet light to turn steady white. Holding it for 15 seconds is a factory reset.
  6. Alternative: restart the router from the Verizon Home app.

Your Verizon Fios equipment

Verizon Router (models CR1000A and CR1000B, Wi-Fi 6E tri-band 2.4/5/6 GHz) on current plans; older installs have the Fios Router (G3100) or the Fios Quantum Gateway (G1100).

Admin page: 192.168.1.1 or myfiosgateway.com (username 'admin', password on the router's rear label), per Verizon Router FAQs and Fios Router FAQs.

App: Verizon Home app (router management: restart, speed test, Wi-Fi networks; rolled out to select routers from Aug 2024). Account management is in the My Verizon app, which replaced the My Fios app on Nov 29, 2023.

Before you call Verizon Fios

Verizon Fios recommends these steps:

  1. Run the Verizon speed test (verizon.com/speedtest) or use the app's Wi-Fi Health Check > Speed Test.
  2. For an accurate test, connect a computer directly to the router with Ethernet, disconnect VPNs, and stop other devices and streaming during the test.
  3. If on Wi-Fi, move your device closer to the router.
  4. Remove other devices from the network and close other applications.
  5. Reboot the router.
  6. Use Verizon's Guided Solutions tool (Fix Slow Internet) for further troubleshooting.
  7. Place the router centrally and away from interference; use 5 GHz for important devices; use WPA2 not WEP; consider a Fios Network Extender for dead zones.

When you do call, bring a record: when it was slow, for how long, and proof your router answered normally the whole time. That moves a ticket past “restart your router.”
