Why is my Spectrum internet slow?

Slow Spectrum internet has two possible sources: your home Wi-Fi, or Spectrum's network. Here's how to tell them apart, the slowdowns typical of Spectrum, and the fixes that work.

By Corey Haines, maker of WiFi or ISP · Updated October 6, 2026

The short answer

Compare your router with the internet. If the router is quick and the internet is slow, it's Spectrum. Check for an outage, restart your equipment in the right order, and if it keeps happening, log it and contact Spectrum with the evidence.

Spectrum's disclosure lists data as Unlimited, with no charges for extra data. Charter completed its acquisition of Cox Communications on August 20, 2026. Spectrum branding, pricing and packaging are rolling out in former Cox markets, and Spectrum's support site now shows a 'Cox is now Spectrum' banner.

First, rule out your Wi-Fi

A slow Spectrum connection and a slow Wi-Fi network feel identical. Time a round trip to your router and one to the internet at the same moment. If your router answers quickly and the internet doesn't, the slowdown is on Spectrum's side. If even your router is slow, it's your Wi-Fi or router, and Spectrum can't fix it. Here's how to check on a Mac.

What usually slows down Spectrum

Spectrum delivers internet over cable (DOCSIS, hybrid fiber-coax), fiber (select areas). The usual causes:

  • Peak-time congestion on the shared neighborhood cable segment (common to DOCSIS cable networks; not specifically stated by Spectrum)
  • Low upload capacity on non-symmetrical tiers (e.g. 20-35 Mbps up), which can bottleneck video calls, cloud backups and uploads
  • An older or uncertified modem that can't deliver your tier: Spectrum keeps an authorized-modem list and offers equipment upgrades when speeds rise
  • Loose or damaged coax and power cables (Spectrum's troubleshooting starts by checking cables)
  • Device-side factors Spectrum lists: file-sharing programs (BitTorrent), viruses/adware/malware, strict firewalls, low system memory (RAM), outdated browser, power-save/low-power mode
  • WiFi limits: Spectrum recommends an 802.11n router for speeds up to 300 Mbps and 802.11ac for speeds above 300 Mbps; 2.4 GHz reaches farthest but is slowest, 6 GHz is fastest but has the shortest range
  • Too many devices in use at once (Spectrum advises disconnecting devices not in use)

Upload versus download

Most standard tiers have much slower upload than download. Spectrum's broadband disclosure lists Spectrum Internet 500 at 500 Mbps x 20 Mbps, Spectrum Internet 1 Gig at 1,000 Mbps x 35 Mbps, and Internet Assist at 50 x 10 Mbps. In select locations Spectrum sells equal ('symmetrical') speeds: Internet 500 up to 300/300 Mbps, Ultra up to 500/500 Mbps and 1 Gig up to 1/1 Gbps. 2 Gig (typical 2,056 down / 1,002 up) and 7 Gig (typical 7,454 down / 7,813 up) are also offered in certain locations. One national label for Internet 100 shows typical 116 Mbps down / 105 Mbps up and 19 ms latency, so upload speeds differ by market and network upgrade status. When an upload fills that smaller pipe, everything else lags, which is called bufferbloat. Test for it.

Check for a Spectrum outage

Spectrum outage page

Spectrum's outage page sends you to chat or a Check Outages button; area outage details are tied to your account (sign in at Spectrum.net or use the My Spectrum App). In the My Spectrum App you can turn on push alerts for outages (More > Settings > Notifications > Manage > Support > Outages). Spectrum also runs a Storm Center page for weather events.

Restart your Spectrum equipment

  1. Online: sign in at Spectrum.net, open the Services tab and then the Internet sub-tab, choose your equipment and select Restart Equipment (you can also do this in the My Spectrum App).
  2. Manual (coax customers only): unplug the power cord from the modem and from the WiFi router.
  3. Wait at least one minute, then reinsert any batteries and plug the modem back in.
  4. Wait at least two more minutes while the modem powers on. Its lights should gradually come on.
  5. Plug the WiFi router back in and give it at least two minutes to finish starting up.
  6. Open two or three different web pages to make sure the connection works.
  7. Fiber customers: Spectrum says not to touch the back of the fiber modem, because that can damage the fiber cable.

Your Spectrum equipment

Spectrum usually supplies a separate modem plus a WiFi router rather than one combo box. Current Advanced WiFi routers (WiFi 5, 6, 6E and 7) have model numbers starting with SAC2V1A, SAC2V2S, SAX1V1R, SAX1V1K, SAX1V1S, SAX2V1S, SAX2V1R, SBE1V1K or SBE1V1R. Only the WiFi 6E and WiFi 7 models (SAX2V1R, SAX2V1S, SBE1V1K) support 6 GHz. Older supported routers listed by Spectrum include the Arris TG1672G, Askey, Netgear 3800/6300, Sagemcom (incl. 5260), Technicolor TC8717T and Ubee DVW32CB.

Admin page: Depends on the router, per Spectrum's WiFi setup article: 192.168.1.1 (Askey, Sagemcom, Sagemcom 5260), 192.168.0.1 (Arris TG1672G, Technicolor TC8717T, Ubee DVW32CB) and www.routerlogin.net (Netgear 3800/6300). Spectrum Advanced WiFi routers are managed through the My Spectrum App or your Spectrum.net account (Services > Internet).

App: My Spectrum App

Your own equipment: Allowed, with limits. You can use a modem you own if it is on Spectrum's authorized retail list. There are separate lists for non-symmetrical tiers up to 1G and up to 500 Mbps, plus a 'minimally qualified' list that Spectrum advises against. Spectrum technicians support only Spectrum-provided equipment. Customer-owned modems do not work with Spectrum Home Phone, and they are allowed only on non-symmetrical tiers: equal-speed customers must use a Spectrum D3.1 modem. Spectrum provides a compliant modem at no extra charge. You can rent a Spectrum WiFi router for a monthly fee or buy your own.

Before you call Spectrum

Spectrum recommends these steps:

  1. Update your device to the latest operating system and browser.
  2. Take a speed test, ideally from a computer wired to the modem with a Cat 5e or better Ethernet cable.
  3. Check for file-sharing programs, malware (Spectrum offers Security Suite), an overly strict firewall, low RAM and power-save mode.
  4. Disconnect devices that aren't in use and restart your computer or device.
  5. Make sure your router suits your speed (802.11n up to 300 Mbps, 802.11ac above 300 Mbps) and that you use a 1 Gbps full-duplex network card.
  6. Restart your modem online (Spectrum.net or the My Spectrum App), or manually: unplug the modem and router, wait 1 minute, power the modem on and wait 2 minutes, then power the router on and wait 2 minutes.
  7. Restart the router by pressing and holding its reset button for five seconds.
  8. If problems continue, contact Spectrum (chat or phone).

When you do call, bring a record: when it was slow, for how long, and proof your router answered normally the whole time. That moves a ticket past “restart your router.”
