Why is my Xfinity internet slow?

Slow Xfinity internet has two possible sources: your home Wi-Fi, or Xfinity's network. Here's how to tell them apart, the slowdowns typical of Xfinity, and the fixes that work.

By Corey Haines, maker of WiFi or ISP · Updated October 6, 2026

The short answer

Compare your router with the internet. If the router is quick and the internet is slow, it's Xfinity. Check for an outage, restart your equipment in the right order, and if it keeps happening, log it and contact Xfinity with the evidence.

Xfinity says its current internet plans have no data caps. Legacy plans may still have a 1.2 TB monthly limit, and accounts over that limit may be charged for extra data. The latest Xfinity plans include the Xfinity Gateway at no extra cost.

First, rule out your Wi-Fi

A slow Xfinity connection and a slow Wi-Fi network feel identical. Time a round trip to your router and one to the internet at the same moment. If your router answers quickly and the internet doesn't, the slowdown is on Xfinity's side. If even your router is slow, it's your Wi-Fi or router, and Xfinity can't fix it. Here's how to check on a Mac.

What usually slows down Xfinity

Xfinity delivers internet over cable (DOCSIS, hybrid fiber-coax), fiber (select communities). The usual causes:

  • Peak-time congestion on the shared neighborhood cable segment (common to DOCSIS cable networks; not specifically stated by Xfinity)
  • Low upload capacity on non-upgraded areas/tiers (as low as ~35 Mbps up on Gigabit), which bottlenecks uploads and video calls
  • Many family members and devices using WiFi at the same time (cited by Xfinity)
  • Devices joining a public xfinitywifi/XFINITY hotspot instead of your home network (cited by Xfinity)
  • Distance from the gateway, and interference from nearby networks, walls, metal, microwaves, cordless phones, baby monitors and Bluetooth devices (cited by Xfinity)
  • Loose coaxial cable connections (Xfinity says to tighten the coax as much as possible)
  • An owned modem that isn't certified for your speed tier or for enhanced upload speeds
  • Outdated devices or operating systems; for Gigabit, wired speeds top out around 940 Mbps over Ethernet (cited by Xfinity)

Upload versus download

Upload depends on whether your area has been upgraded. On the standard network upload is far below download: Xfinity's Gigabit article lists download up to 1000 Mbps (1200 in some areas) and upload up to 35 Mbps. Since 2022 Xfinity has been moving areas to 'enhanced speed' status with 5-10x faster uploads (75-200 Mbps). X-Class tiers offer equal upload and download above 200 Mbps in limited areas. When an upload fills that smaller pipe, everything else lags, which is called bufferbloat. Test for it.

Check for a Xfinity outage

Xfinity outage page

The Xfinity Outage Map asks you to sign in for details about your service. Outage updates are also available in the Xfinity app and through Xfinity Assistant, which can find outages and run diagnostics.

Restart your Xfinity equipment

  1. In the Xfinity app (rented gateways): open the WiFi section, tap Troubleshoot, then select Restart your Gateway.
  2. Or run the same troubleshooting from Xfinity Assistant or xfinity.com on a computer.
  3. Manual: unplug the gateway's power cable, wait one minute and plug it back in.
  4. The restart can take up to 12 minutes, and your home network won't work in the meantime. With Xfinity Voice, you can't make or receive calls (including 911) until the gateway is back online.

Your Xfinity equipment

Xfinity WiFi Gateway, an all-in-one modem and router. Models in Xfinity's user manuals: XB3, XB6, XB7, XB8, XB9 and XB10. Also listed: the Xfinity WiFi Router XER10, the XE2 WiFi Extender and the XD4 Advanced Modem.

Admin page: http://10.0.0.1 (Xfinity Admin Tool). You first have to allow access in the Xfinity app: WiFi tab > View WiFi equipment > Advanced settings > Admin Tool online access > Allow, then Save. The username is admin, and the password is the one you create when you enable access. People who enabled access before February 2026 may still use the default password on the gateway's sticker.

App: Xfinity app

Bridge mode: Yes. Bridge Mode turns off the gateway's routing and leaves the modem running so you can use your own router. You enable it in the Admin Tool at http://10.0.0.1 under Gateway > At a Glance > Bridge Mode: Enable (Admin Tool access may need to be allowed in the Xfinity app first). Caveats: only one device can connect to the gateway in Bridge Mode. You can't manage your network in the app or use WiFi Extenders, and Bridge Mode won't turn on if extenders are already activated. Xfinity CyberSecure access is blocked.

Your own equipment: Allowed if the device works with Xfinity and supports your speed. Check it in My Device Info (sign in or enter your address). Most modems need a separate router for WiFi. You are responsible for installing it, keeping it updated and troubleshooting it, and unsupported devices won't work. Customer-owned modems don't currently work with X-Class (equal upload/download) speeds. Only a few retail devices support the faster upload speeds in upgraded 'enhanced' areas. Xfinity Prepaid and NOW Internet equipment doesn't work with regular Xfinity Internet. In fiber-delivered communities, any standard router that supports your speed tier can be used.

Before you call Xfinity

Xfinity recommends these steps:

  1. Use the Xfinity app (WiFi > Troubleshoot) or Xfinity Assistant to check for outages and run diagnostics.
  2. Check several devices to see whether the problem affects one device or the whole network.
  3. Try a few different apps or websites to rule out a problem with a single service.
  4. Make sure you're within WiFi range of the gateway or router.
  5. Confirm the equipment cables are plugged in and the coaxial cable is fully tightened.
  6. Run a speed test on a single device to check download and upload speeds.
  7. Make sure devices are on your home network, not an xfinitywifi hotspot, and not paused or blocked in the Xfinity app.
  8. Plug high-bandwidth devices into the gateway with Ethernet. Place the gateway centrally, upright and off the floor, away from interference.
  9. Restart the gateway (app or unplug for one minute). If the problem can't be fixed online, schedule a technician.

When you do call, bring a record: when it was slow, for how long, and proof your router answered normally the whole time. That moves a ticket past “restart your router.”
