Why is my CenturyLink internet slow?

Slow CenturyLink internet has two possible sources: your home Wi-Fi, or CenturyLink's network. Here's how to tell them apart, the slowdowns typical of CenturyLink, and the fixes that work.

By Corey Haines, maker of WiFi or ISP · Updated October 6, 2026

The short answer

Compare your router with the internet. If the router is quick and the internet is slow, it's CenturyLink. Check for an outage, restart your equipment in the right order, and if it keeps happening, log it and contact CenturyLink with the evidence.

AT&T completed its acquisition of substantially all of Lumen's Mass Markets fiber business, including Quantum Fiber, on February 2, 2026 ($5.75B; more than 1 million fiber subscribers across more than 4 million locations in 11 states). Lumen says it kept its copper-based consumer services, so CenturyLink DSL stays with Lumen. Quantum Fiber's site says it is 'now part of the AT&T family' and that existing customers keep their equipment and manage service in the same Quantum Fiber app/portal. Quantum Fiber-specific support URLs were not researched. CenturyLink advertises unlimited data and no data caps on DSL. Contact page offers 7-day chat; phone numbers shown are region-dependent (800-244-1111 and 800-201-4099).

First, rule out your Wi-Fi

A slow CenturyLink connection and a slow Wi-Fi network feel identical. Time a round trip to your router and one to the internet at the same moment. If your router answers quickly and the internet doesn't, the slowdown is on CenturyLink's side. If even your router is slow, it's your Wi-Fi or router, and CenturyLink can't fix it. Here's how to check on a Mac.

What usually slows down CenturyLink

CenturyLink delivers internet over DSL (ADSL / ADSL2+ / VDSL, including pair-bonded lines), fiber (FTTH). The usual causes:

  • A problem on the DSL line itself. CenturyLink says to check the Downstream Rate in Modem Status at 192.168.0.1: if it is well below your plan speed, it is a service issue that may need a technician; if it is close, the cause is likely Wi-Fi, household traffic or devices.
  • Missing DSL filters on landline phones when you have both internet and CenturyLink home phone, or a loose DSL cord (often green) between the wall jack and the modem's DSL port.
  • A modem that has been running a long time and needs a reboot (which also lets it pick the least-interfered Wi-Fi channel), or outdated modem firmware.
  • Wi-Fi 'piggybacking' by neighbors on a weakly secured network.
  • Interference from microwaves, cordless phones, Bluetooth devices, TVs and baby monitors, plus physical obstacles such as large appliances and metal or glass objects.
  • Older devices that cannot reach high speeds and can slow the whole network, and always-on devices running background updates.
  • Peak hours when more household members are online, and busy content providers or web hosts.
  • Rural locations, which CenturyLink says often get slower speeds than urban centers.

Upload versus download

CenturyLink says that on DSL plans the download speed tends to be much higher than the upload speed, while fiber often offers symmetrical speeds. Tested DSL speeds on the C4000: ADSL up to 7 Mbps, ADSL2+ up to 20 Mbps, VDSL up to 100 Mbps, and bonded pairs up to 40 Mbps (ADSL2+) or 140 Mbps (VDSL). Fiber tested up to 940 Mbps. CenturyLink DSL is marketed at up to 100 Mbps. When an upload fills that smaller pipe, everything else lags, which is called bufferbloat. Test for it.

Check for a CenturyLink outage

CenturyLink outage page

Requires sign-in: when you sign in to My CenturyLink on the web or in the app, you see an alert if you are affected by an outage, and you can sign up for outage notifications there. The automated Troubleshooter tool also tests your line and equipment. CenturyLink says to always reboot your modem after an outage.

Restart your CenturyLink equipment

  1. Unplug the power cord from the back of the modem or from the wall outlet, and check that all the modem lights go off.
  2. Wait 30 seconds to 1 minute (at least 30 seconds so the modem memory fully clears).
  3. Plug the power cord back in and wait for the power and internet lights to turn green (or the single status light on the C4000). A full boot can take 3-5 minutes.
  4. Alternatively, in the My CenturyLink app go to Services and tap the restart modem button (top tasks area or the Equipment section). On the web, go to My CenturyLink > Services > Equipment > your modem > Restart Modem.
  5. Fiber with Premium WiFi: unplug the SmartNID power cord, wait at least 30 seconds, plug it back in and wait 2-3 minutes. Do not remove the fiber cords from the SmartNID.

Your CenturyLink equipment

CenturyLink gateways (all-in-one modem/router). Current recommended models: C4000 (C4000LG/LZ for ADSL/ADSL2+/VDSL, C4000BG/BZ for bonded-pair DSL, C4000XG for fiber), C3510XZ (fiber) and C3000. Other certified models in use: C2300A, C5500XK Fiber SmartNID, PK5000Z, C424G, C844G. CenturyLink Fiber with Premium WiFi uses a wall-mounted SmartNID plus WiFi pods.

Admin page: http://192.168.0.1 (modem settings GUI). Sign in with the Admin Username and Admin Password printed on the modem sticker.

App: My CenturyLink app (shows outage alerts, can restart the modem, and manages Premium WiFi). Quantum Fiber customers use the Quantum Fiber app.

Bridge mode: Yes, called transparent bridging. On newer CenturyLink modems, set Transparent Bridging in the Advanced Setup > WAN Settings of the modem GUI. You must chat with tech support to get the transport mode (VLAN setting) for your area, and it is not available in all areas. CenturyLink warns not to change WAN settings unless a CenturyLink technician or IT professional tells you to.

Your own equipment: Allowed, with caveats. A third-party modem must support VLAN tagging 201, and modems from cable internet providers do not work. CenturyLink says most third-party modems don't work, or don't work well, and it publishes no list of compatible third-party models. You get only limited tech support, no equipment replacement, and you need PPPoE username/password and VPI/VCI from chat. Technicians install new service with a CenturyLink modem; you can return it afterward if your own modem works. Re-using a newer CenturyLink modem that is compatible with your connection type and speed is likely to work.

Before you call CenturyLink

CenturyLink recommends these steps:

  1. Check your connection status: sign in to the modem settings GUI and compare the Downstream Rate in Modem Status to your plan speed.
  2. Reboot your modem.
  3. Make sure your WiFi network is secured with a strong password.
  4. For fiber, make sure you have the right equipment for fiber speeds.
  5. Improve Wi-Fi: reduce interference and move the router away from obstacles and dense appliances.
  6. Check devices: older devices, background updates, malware and too many running applications can slow things down.
  7. Check cables: the DSL cord should be in a working phone jack and the modem's DSL port, and DSL filters should be on all landline phones.
  8. Try a wired Ethernet connection directly to the modem.
  9. Run a speed test and compare it to your plan speed in My CenturyLink.
  10. If speeds stay below your plan, run the automated Troubleshooter (which can open a repair ticket and schedule a technician) or contact a support agent.

When you do call, bring a record: when it was slow, for how long, and proof your router answered normally the whole time. That moves a ticket past “restart your router.”
