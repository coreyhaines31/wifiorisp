How the lookup works

Every internet provider in the US must tell the FCC which homes it can serve, with which technology, and at what top advertised speeds. The lookup uses the FCC's public release of that data for homes, not businesses. It finds the neighborhood your address is in (about a third of a square mile) and lists each provider that serves it, with the share of nearby homes it covers.

A listing means the provider says it can serve the area, not that it serves every home in it, and the data has no prices. To check one exact address, enter it on the FCC's National Broadband Map, then confirm with the provider before you count on it.

How much internet speed do you need?

Less than most plans sell you, unless you upload a lot. These are rough needs with room to spare, the same math the lookup uses to mark which plans fit.

HomeDownloadUpload
1 person, HD streaming and browsing25 Mbps5 Mbps
2 people, video calls, one 4K stream50 Mbps6 Mbps
4 people, calls, two 4K streams, gaming110 Mbps12 Mbps
1 person working from home, with big uploads (video, backups)25 Mbps23 Mbps

Upload is where plans fall short. Cable plans often top out at 20 to 100 Mbps up while fiber is usually the same speed both ways.

Before you switch

Make sure the provider is the problem. If your router answers slowly, a new provider won't help. The WiFi or ISP app tells you which side it is, and if you're getting far less than you pay for, its report helps you get that fixed first. Look up your current provider's problems in our slow internet guides by provider, and test for lag with the bufferbloat test.

When you compare plans, read the FCC broadband label every provider must show: the price after the promotion ends, equipment fees, and data caps.

Questions

How do I find internet providers near me?

Enter your address in the lookup at the top of this page. It lists every provider that reports serving your neighborhood to the FCC, with technology and top speeds, and marks which plans fit your home.

Which internet providers are in my area?

It depends on the street. Most US homes can get cable and a 5G home service, many can get fiber, and almost every home can get satellite. The lookup shows the ones at your address.

How much internet speed do I need?

A rough guide: 10 Mbps down per person, plus 25 Mbps for each 4K stream, and about 3 Mbps up for each person on a video call. Two people working from home need about 50 Mbps down and 6 Mbps up.

Is fiber better than cable?

Usually. Fiber offers the same speed up and down and lower, steadier latency. Cable is fast downstream but uploads are much smaller, and it can slow down in busy evenings.

Where does this data come from?

From the FCC's Broadband Data Collection, which every provider must file. The lookup uses the latest public release, covering homes as of December 31, 2025. It shows what providers say they offer, not prices.

Know whether it's the provider before you switch.

WiFi or ISP is a free Mac menu bar app. It times your router and the internet at the same moment, says which side is slow, and keeps a log you can send your ISP.
